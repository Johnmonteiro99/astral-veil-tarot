The Architecture of the Arcana

Understanding the Tarot Deck

A traditional tarot deck contains 78 cards divided into two interconnected groups: the Major Arcana and the Minor Arcana. Together, they explore both life’s defining transformations and the quieter experiences that shape each day.

Major Arcana and Minor Arcana

22

Major Arcana

The 22 Major Arcana represent archetypes, turning points, and larger chapters of personal growth. Their journey begins with The Fool and moves through choice, loss, awakening, transformation, and completion.

56

Minor Arcana

The 56 Minor Arcana bring tarot into everyday life. Through four suits, they explore action, emotion, thought, relationships, work, security, conflict, and change.

22 + 56 Together, these two currents form all 78 tarot cards.

The Four Tarot Suits

The suits are the language of everyday experience. Each holds a world of meaning: an element, a purpose, and a path.

Wands

Fire

The spark of will and creative power. Wands ignite passion, action, ambition, and the courage to create.

Cups

Water

The flow of emotion and intuition. Cups reveal relationships, compassion, imagination, and the depths of feeling.

Swords

Air

The blade of mind and truth. Swords bring clarity, communication, discernment, and the strength to cut through illusion.

Pentacles

Earth

The realm of matter and manifestation. Pentacles ground dreams into form through work, stability, resources, and long-term growth.

Upright and Reversed Tarot Cards

A card’s orientation can change how its energy is understood. Upright and reversed meanings are not simply “good” and “bad.” They can reflect expression, resistance, imbalance, delay, internalization, or transformation.

Upright

How the card’s energy is expressed, available, developing, or moving forward.

Tarot cards are a symbolic system of 78 illustrated cards used for reflection, storytelling, intuition, and divination. Each card carries traditional meanings, but its message also changes through the question being asked, the surrounding cards, and the reader’s interpretation.

A traditional tarot deck contains 78 cards. It is divided into 22 Major Arcana cards and 56 Minor Arcana cards. The Minor Arcana is further divided into four suits: Wands, Cups, Swords, and Pentacles.

The Major Arcana explores larger archetypes, turning points, and transformative chapters of life. The Minor Arcana focuses on everyday experiences such as emotions, relationships, choices, conflict, work, security, and personal growth.

An upright card often reflects energy that is available, expressed, developing, or moving forward. A reversed card can point to resistance, delay, imbalance, internalization, excess, or a message that needs to be understood differently. Reversed does not automatically mean negative.

Tarot can illuminate patterns, possibilities, and the direction a situation may be moving, but it does not remove uncertainty or choice. Astral Veil approaches tarot as a reflection of the present rather than an unchangeable verdict about the future.
