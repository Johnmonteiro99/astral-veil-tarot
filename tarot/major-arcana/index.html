The 22 Archetypes of the Soul

Major Arcana Explained

The Major Arcana contains twenty-two cards that represent the larger patterns of human experience: beginnings, choices, relationships, challenges, transformation, and completion.

Numbered from The Fool at 0 to The World at 21, these cards often point to defining lessons and turning points. Together, they create a symbolic journey through growth, awareness, and change.

Foundations

What Is the Major Arcana?

The Major Arcana is a group of twenty-two cards within a standard seventy-eight-card tarot deck. Unlike the Minor Arcana, which often focuses on daily events and practical situations, the Major Arcana addresses broader archetypes, life lessons, and defining periods of change.

The sequence begins with The Fool, numbered 0, and ends with The World, numbered 21. Each card can stand alone, but the complete sequence is often read as a symbolic path through experience, identity, challenge, transformation, and integration.

A Major Arcana card does not automatically predict a dramatic event. Its meaning depends on the question, the surrounding cards, the spread position, and the reader’s interpretive approach.

A Teaching Framework

Understanding the Fool’s Journey

Readers often use the Fool’s Journey as a teaching framework for understanding how the Major Arcana connects. It follows The Fool through encounters that represent development, challenge, self-knowledge, transformation, and return.

The Fool’s Journey is a modern interpretive framework rather than an ancient historical doctrine.

Chapter I · 0–7

Entering the World

The Fool through The Chariot

The Chapter Story

The opening cards introduce identity, personal ability, intuition, care, structure, shared beliefs, relationship, and directed action. This chapter explores how the individual begins participating in the world.

The Fool begins as unformed potential, carrying openness but little certainty about the road ahead. Through The Magician, potential becomes intentional action. The High Priestess introduces inward knowledge, while The Empress and The Emperor reveal complementary forms of creation, care, structure, and authority.

The Hierophant introduces shared teaching and inherited belief. The Lovers brings values, relationship, and meaningful choice into focus. The Chariot closes the chapter by asking the traveler to gather these influences into a chosen direction.

This chapter is not simply about childhood or beginnings. It explores how identity forms through the meeting of inner ability, external guidance, relationships, responsibilities, and conscious decisions.

The Central Lesson

Entering the world means learning how to act without abandoning intuition, receive guidance without surrendering discernment, and choose direction without denying complexity.

In a Reading

A concentration of cards from this chapter may emphasize identity formation, new participation, relationship choices, personal agency, or the need to establish direction. Interpretation still depends on the question, position, and surrounding cards.

Chapter II · 8–14

Meeting the Inner Self

Strength through Temperance

The Chapter Story

The middle sequence turns inward through courage, solitude, changing cycles, accountability, surrender, transformation, and integration. It examines how inner development changes our relationship with experience.

Strength shifts the journey from visible control toward emotional courage and compassionate self-command. The Hermit withdraws from external noise, and the Wheel of Fortune reveals that not every change can be controlled. Justice asks for accountability, while The Hanged Man interrupts momentum so a different perspective can emerge.

Death brings necessary ending and transformation. Temperance completes the chapter through gradual balance, healing, and integration.

The traveler is no longer learning only how to participate in the world. The challenge becomes understanding internal reactions, limits, patterns, and responsibilities.

The Central Lesson

Inner development requires the courage to pause, evaluate, release, and combine what experience has revealed.

In a Reading

Several cards from this chapter may point toward reflection, accountability, transition, patience, or an internal process that cannot be resolved through force alone.

Chapter III · 15–21

Transformation and Return

The Devil through The World

The Chapter Story

The final chapter confronts attachment, disruption, hope, uncertainty, illumination, awakening, and completion. It describes the difficult work of recognizing patterns and returning with deeper awareness.

The Devil exposes attachment, fear, excess, and the places where choice has been obscured. The Tower disrupts unstable structures, while The Star restores hope and authenticity after collapse. The Moon leads through uncertainty and subconscious material before The Sun brings illumination and vitality.

Judgement asks the traveler to understand the past and answer a more conscious calling. The World completes the sequence through integration, but completion is not static perfection. It is the capacity to carry the entire journey forward with greater awareness.

The Central Lesson

Transformation requires confronting what binds us, surviving the loss of false certainty, and returning to life with a more integrated understanding.

In a Reading

Several cards from this chapter may emphasize release, revelation, recovery, awakening, completion, or a period in which old structures and identities are being transformed.

Two Ways Energy Appears

Upright and Reversed Major Arcana Meanings

☼

Upright

Expression in the World

An upright card often shows the archetype in a more direct, external, or available expression. It may describe energy that is consciously recognized or actively unfolding.

  • ✦Direct expression
  • ✦Available energy
  • ✦Visible development
  • ✦Conscious participation
☾

Reversed

Energy Turned Inward

A reversed card is not automatically negative. Depending on the reader and context, it may describe internalized energy, resistance, delay, imbalance, avoidance, or a lesson that needs closer attention.

  • ✦Internal expression
  • ✦Resistance or delay
  • ✦Imbalance
  • ✦Shadow or reflection
